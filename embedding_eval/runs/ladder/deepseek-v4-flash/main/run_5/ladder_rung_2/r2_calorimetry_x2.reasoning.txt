We need to find final equilibrium temperature of a mixture of various substances in an insulated container. All ice melts and all steam condenses, final temperature between 0 and 100 °C. So we have to account for phase changes. We can compute total energy change: sum of heat gained/lost by each component to reach final temperature T. Since container is insulated, total energy change = 0. So we set up equation: sum over all bodies of (heat required to bring them from initial state to final state) = 0, where we consider phase changes if needed.

We have solids that do not change phase (granite, glass, tin, brass, aluminium, and also water in various phases: ice, liquid water, steam). For each we need to compute heat transfer.

Let's define final temperature T in °C (but we'll use Celsius, since specific heats are given in J/(kg·K) and temperature differences are same in °C and K). We'll use Q = m c ΔT for sensible heat, and for phase changes: melting ice at 0°C: Q = m L_f (positive for melting, i.e., heat absorbed); condensation of steam at 100°C: Q = - m L_v (since steam releases heat when condensing). Our approach: For each substance, compute net heat transfer from initial to final state, assuming that all ice becomes water at T, all steam becomes water at T, and any liquid water initially is just heated/cooled to T. Also solids are heated/cooled to T.

Since T is between 0 and 100, water remains liquid, no further phase changes.

Let's list all 18 bodies with their masses, initial temperatures, and specific heats (if solid) and phase.

We'll compute Q_i = m_i * c_i * (T - T_i) for substances that don't change phase (solids and liquid water). For ice: we need to warm ice from initial T_i to 0°C, melt it, then warm resulting water from 0 to T. For steam: cool steam from initial T_i to 100°C, condense it, then cool resulting water from 100 to T. For liquid water: just Q = m c_w (T - T_i). For solids: Q = m c_s (T - T_i). Then sum Q_i = 0.

We have to be careful with signs: Q_i positive if heat is absorbed by the body, negative if it releases heat. In the equation sum Q_i = 0, we can compute each Q_i as heat gained (positive if temperature increases, negative if decreases). Then solve for T.
