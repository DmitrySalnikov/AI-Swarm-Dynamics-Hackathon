We need to compute the moment of inertia of a composite body about an axis perpendicular to the xy-plane through the point (-2, 0). The body consists of 52 parts: point masses, disks, rings, rods, rectangular plates. They are all rigidly joined, and they may overlap, so we just sum their individual moments of inertia about the given axis, using the parallel axis theorem. For each part, we need its mass, its moment of inertia about its own center of mass (CM) for an axis perpendicular to the plane, and then the distance from its CM to the given axis to apply parallel axis: I = I_cm + m*d^2, where d is the distance between the CM of the part and the axis point (-2,0). Since all parts are in the plane, the perpendicular axis is just through that point. For rods, they are thin and lie in the plane; the moment of inertia about a perpendicular axis through the rod's center is mL^2/12 (as given). For disks (solid), I_cm = mR^2/2. For rings (thin hoop), I_cm = mR^2. For rectangular plates (thin, sides parallel to axes), I_cm = m(a^2 + b^2)/12, where a and b are side lengths. For point masses, I_cm=0.
