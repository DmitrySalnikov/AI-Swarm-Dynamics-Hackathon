We need to compute moment of inertia (MoI) of the entire composite body about an axis perpendicular to xy-plane through point (2,0). The body consists of 52 parts (some may overlap, but they are all rigidly joined, so we just sum moments of inertia of each part about that axis). The parts include thin uniform rods (line segments), uniform solid disks, thin uniform rings (hoops), thin uniform rectangular plates, and point masses. For each part, we need its mass and its moment of inertia about the axis through (2,0). We can use standard formulas for MoI about its own center of mass (CM) axis perpendicular to plane, then use parallel-axis theorem to shift to axis through (2,0). Since all parts are in the plane, the axis perpendicular to plane is same for all. So for each part, we compute I_total = I_cm + m * d^2, where d is distance from the part's CM to the point (2,0). For rods, we need the MoI about its own CM for a thin rod of length L: I_cm = (1/12) m L^2. For solid disk: I_cm = (1/2) m R^2. For thin ring: I_cm = m R^2. For rectangular plate with sides a and b (parallel to x,y axes): I_cm = (1/12) m (a^2 + b^2). For point mass: I_cm = 0, so I = m d^2.

We need to compute for each part: find its mass m, its center of mass coordinates (x_cm, y_cm), its relevant geometric parameters (length L for rod, radius R for disk/ring, dimensions a,b for rectangle), then compute I_cm, then compute d^2 = (x_cm - 2)^2 + (y_cm - 0)^2. Then add to total.
