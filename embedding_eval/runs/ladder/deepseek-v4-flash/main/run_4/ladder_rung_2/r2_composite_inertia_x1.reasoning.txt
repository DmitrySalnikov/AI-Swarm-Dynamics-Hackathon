We are asked to find moment of inertia of a composite rigid body about an axis perpendicular to xy-plane through point (-2,0). The body consists of 52 parts: point masses, solid disks, thin rings (hoops), thin rods, and rectangular plates. All parts are rigidly joined, they may overlap. We need to compute total moment of inertia about given axis.

We are given standard moments of inertia about axis through their own center of mass (perpendicular to plane) for each shape: thin rod: (1/12) m L^2, solid disk: (1/2) m R^2, thin ring: m R^2, rectangular plate: m (a^2+b^2)/12 (where a and b are side lengths). Then we need to add parallel axis theorem: I = I_cm + m d^2, where d is distance from part's center of mass to the axis point (-2,0). For point masses, just m d^2.

We need to compute for each part: its mass m, its center of mass (for rods, center is midpoint; for disks/rings/plates, center is given as centre; for point masses, location given). Then compute squared distance from (-2,0) to that center: d^2 = (x_c - (-2))^2 + (y_c - 0)^2 = (x_c+2)^2 + y_c^2. Also for rods and plates, we need I_cm about axis through their own center perpendicular to plane. For rods, length L is distance between endpoints; for plate, a and b are side lengths; for disk, radius R; for ring, radius R.

We must sum up contributions. Overlap is allowed, but we treat each part separately. So total I = sum (I_cm,i + m_i d_i^2). We need exact value as reduced fraction or integer.
