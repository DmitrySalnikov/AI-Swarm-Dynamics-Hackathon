We need to compute the moment of inertia about an axis perpendicular to the xy-plane through the point (-2, 0). That axis is parallel to the z-axis. The moment of inertia of a system of particles and rigid bodies about a given axis is the sum of the moments of inertia of each component about that axis. Since the axis is perpendicular to the plane, for each part we can compute its moment of inertia about the given axis using the parallel axis theorem: I = I_cm + M * d^2, where I_cm is the moment of inertia about its own center of mass (about a parallel axis through its CM), M is mass, and d is the distance from the component's CM to the axis point (-2,0). For point masses, I_cm = 0. For extended bodies, we need I_cm: for a thin rod of length L, about axis perpendicular to rod through its center: I_cm = mL^2/12; for a solid disk of radius R: I_cm = mR^2/2; for a thin ring (hoop): I_cm = mR^2; for a rectangular plate of sides a and b (parallel to x and y axes), about axis through its center perpendicular to plate: I_cm = m(a^2 + b^2)/12. Note: The rods are thin; the given ends are coordinates, so length can be computed. The rectangular plates have sides parallel to axes; their dimensions given as a x b, with a along x? The problem says sides parallel to x and y axes, so a and b are the side lengths in x and y directions respectively. So we can compute.

We have 52 parts. We need to compute for each: mass m, coordinates of its center of mass (CM), I_cm (if extended), distance squared from CM to point (-2,0): d^2 = (x_cm - (-2))^2 + (y_cm - 0)^2 = (x_cm + 2)^2 + y_cm^2. Then I = I_cm + m * d^2. Sum all.

We must be careful: Overlap is allowed; they are rigidly joined, so we just sum moments.
