We need to find the total moment of inertia about an axis perpendicular to the xy-plane through point (2,0). The body consists of many parts, each with specified geometry, mass, dimensions, and location. We need to compute the moment of inertia of each part about that axis and sum them. The parts may overlap, but they are rigidly joined; we treat them as separate contributions. Overlap is allowed, so we just sum contributions.

We can use standard formulas for moment of inertia about an axis through the center of mass perpendicular to the plane, then use parallel axis theorem: I = I_cm + m * d^2, where d is distance from the part's center of mass to the axis point (2,0). For rods, we have to be careful: the rod is thin uniform, endpoints given; its center of mass is at the midpoint. Its moment of inertia about an axis through its center of mass perpendicular to the plane is mL²/12, where L is length. For disks: I_cm = mR²/2. For rings (hoops): I_cm = mR². For rectangular plates: sides parallel to axes, dimensions a and b (maybe a along x, b along y or vice versa, but the formula I_cm = m(a²+b²)/12 works for any orientation if a and b are side lengths, because it's about an axis through the center perpendicular to the plate). For point masses: I = m d² directly.

Thus for each part, we compute:
- Mass m.
- Coordinates of its center of mass (x_cm, y_cm).
- Distance squared from (2,0): d² = (x_cm-2)² + (y_cm-0)².
- I_cm (the moment of inertia about its own center of mass axis perpendicular to plane).
- Then contribution I = I_cm + m * d².
