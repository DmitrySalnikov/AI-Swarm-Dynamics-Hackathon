Interpretation: We have a flow from section 1 to section 2 to ... to section 6. For each pair, the pressure drop due to losses is K_i * (ρ v_i^2/2), where v_i is the velocity in section i (the upstream). This loss is applied at the connection. In between sections, we assume that Bernoulli's equation (including the dynamic pressure and hydrostatic terms) holds except for these discrete losses. So we can apply the extended Bernoulli equation from section i to section i+1:

p_i + (1/2)ρ v_i^2 + ρ g z_i = p_{i+1} + (1/2)ρ v_{i+1}^2 + ρ g z_{i+1} + Δp_loss,i

where Δp_loss,i = K_i * (1/2)ρ v_i^2.

Actually careful: The given: "Between section i and section i + 1 the flow loses pressure Δp_loss = K·ρ·v_i²/2, where v_i is the speed in the upstream section i; otherwise the Bernoulli equation holds: p + ρv²/2 + ρgz is conserved." So you have Bernoulli conservation from before the loss to after the loss? Usually, loss occurs as a reduction in total pressure. So we can write:

Total pressure at section i (p_i + (1/2)ρv_i^2 + ρgz_i) = Total pressure at section i+1 (p_{i+1} + (1/2)ρv_{i+1}^2 + ρgz_{i+1}) + Δp_loss,i

Thus, p_i + (1/2)ρv_i^2 + ρgz_i = p_{i+1} + (1/2)ρv_{i+1}^2 + ρgz_{i+1} + K_i * (1/2)ρv_i^2
