We are given 18 bodies with different masses, initial temperatures, and specific heats (for solids) or phases (for water/ice/steam). They are brought together in an insulated container, and we are told that at final equilibrium, all ice has melted and all steam has condensed, so the final state is all liquid water (for the water components) plus the solids at some final temperature T between 0 and 100°C. So we need to compute the final temperature by energy conservation: total energy change = 0.

We must include latent heats for phase changes: ice melts (fusion), steam condenses (vaporization). Also need to heat or cool the various materials to the final temperature T.

We'll compute the net heat gained (or lost) by each body as it goes from its initial state to final state (all liquid water at T for water-related substances, and solids at T). The sum of all Q must be zero.

Let's list all bodies with their properties. We'll compute Q_i = m_i * c_i * (T - T_i) for solids and for water in liquid state if it stays liquid; for ice: need to warm ice to 0°C, melt it, then warm the resulting water to T; for steam: need to cool steam to 100°C, condense it, then cool the resulting water to T.

We'll use Celsius temperatures, but careful with specific heats in J/(kg·K). Since temperature differences in Celsius and Kelvin are same, we can use °C directly.

We'll compute:

For ice: Q = m * [c_ice*(0 - T_i) + L_f + c_water*(T - 0)] = m * (c_ice*(-T_i) + L_f + c_water*T) since T_i is negative. For steam: Q = m * [c_steam*(100 - T_i) + (-L_v) + c_water*(T - 100)]? Actually steam condenses: it releases latent heat, so heat lost is negative. We'll compute Q_i = A_i + B_i * T, where B_i is the coefficient for T (the heat capacity of the final state for that body). For bodies that end as liquid water, the final heat capacity is m * c_water (since final water). For solids, final heat capacity is m * c_solid. For ice, it's m * c_water (since final water). For water (from ice, steam, or initial liquid) it's c_water = 4186 J/(kg·K). However, careful: For ice and steam, there are also contributions from phase changes and temperature changes to reach 0°C or 100°C that are constant. For solids: Q = m*c*(T - T_i) = -m*c*T_i + m*c*T. So constant = -m*c*T_i, coefficient = m*c.

Thus total Q = Σ (constant_i) + T * Σ (coefficient_i) = 0 => T = - (Σ constant_i) / (Σ coefficient_i).
